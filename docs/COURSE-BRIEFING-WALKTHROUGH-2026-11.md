# Course briefing walkthrough: one sheet

For Ethan, with one organiser at a time. About 20 minutes. It is a sheet to use, not a feature. Background:
`docs/COURSE-BRIEFING-HENLEY-2026-11.md`. The interview rules in `docs/ORGANISER-INTERVIEWS-2026-11.md` §3 still
apply: their routine first, no price unless they ask, notes in their words, nothing quoted without asking.

**Pass test.** Across three walkthroughs, at least two organisers name one of these: a checkpoint they would add,
an extra check they would make, or a change they would communicate. "Interesting" does not count.

## Before

1. Serve the repository root: `python3 -m http.server 8793`
2. Open two tabs. Tab A is the live spot page, `https://swimsignal.co.uk/spot/thames-henley/`. Tab B is
   `http://localhost:8793/prototypes/course-briefing/henley-classic/`. Keep B hidden until step 2 of each date.
3. Say three things: "This is a prototype. Nobody has published it." "These are three past dates, not today."
   "It is a forecast and a map of evidence, not a water test. You decide."

The likeliest mistake is showing tab B first. Their answer to "what would you do" is then shaped by it, and the
comparison is lost.

## For each date

Do them in this order. Read step 1 aloud, then wait.

| | 1. What you could have seen | 2. Then open tab B on | 3. What the course view adds |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A. Sunday 1 November 2026, 08:40** | The Henley page said low risk. Index 1 of 100. All 49 overflows upstream reporting, none spilling. | `#date=routine` | Nothing. Same evidence along all 2.1 km. Say so: a quiet day is a true picture. |
| **B. Saturday 6 July 2024, 13:00** | SwimSignal issued nothing in 2024. Ask what they used that day, if anything. | `#date=differs` | Henley sewage works recorded spilling 11:45 to 12:45. It is upstream of the first 350 m only. The next 510 m is unresolved. |
| **C. Saturday 14 November 2026, 07:30** | The Henley page said low risk. Index 3. 48 of 49 reporting. | `#date=gap` | The missing one is Friday Street pumping station, 300 m above the finish. It spilled for 4 minutes the night before, then gave no reading all day. |

After step 1, ask: **"With only that, what would you have done for a swim on this course?"**

After step 3, ask, in this order:

1. "Does this change anything you would have done? What?"
2. "Is there a point on the course where you would add a check? Where, and what check?"
3. "Would you tell anyone something different: swimmers, safety crew, the landowner?"
4. "What here do you not believe, or not need?"

Say for date B, before they answer: "These spill times were published after the year ended. Nobody could have
read them that day. The live feed could have shown a spill, if someone was looking."

## If they ask

- **"Is the water at the start worse?"** Not known. The page counts which overflows are upstream. It measures
  nothing, and gives no E. coli figure on a river this size.
- **"Why is the middle section unresolved?"** The map shows ditches that may link the works' stream to a second
  mouth. Nobody has checked on the ground.
- **"Can I have this for my event?"** It is a prototype for one course. Note the request and the event. Promise
  nothing.
