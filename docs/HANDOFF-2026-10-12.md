is not yet reliable enough to recommend. They gave four faults at Lake District spots, all true:

- Loughrigg Tarn took Grasmere's overflows.
- A lake on the way was crossed as river.
- There is no wind term.
- The level counts sewage overflows only.

That led to three checks:

- a route audit of every spot;
- a comparison with the Environment Agency's bathing water profiles and zones of influence for all 38 designated bathing waters;
- a red-team read of every page type.

Together they found about 15 wrong statements, about 25 misleading ones, and a dozen routing errors. **Do not reply to the reviewer until section 2's checks pass on the live site.** Ethan holds the reply draft privately, not in this repo.

## 4. How to improve each fix

These are the weak points known so far, ordered by how much they matter to a group leader.

**A. The length dilution itself (the root of several faults).**
- Weight is (upstream length at the overflow + 5 km) / (upstream length at the spot + 5 km). So an overflow on a short ditch beside a big river gets almost nothing, though its spill reaches the river whole: Culham STW was 0.005 before its join, Benson STW is 0.006, Oxford STW 0.007 on Littlemore Brook. The reviewer from Sheffield said this has "no logic".
- Three alternatives have failed their pre-stated tests: flow dilution, urban area (#166, #172) and junction (#181).
- #172 found that the urban proxy gives the wrong sizes, and that a fair test needs an independent spill size per overflow plus a permutation control.
- Next candidates:
  - spill hours from the annual returns as the size term;
  - population equivalent where the EA's consents give it.
- State the test before it is run, as before.

**B. Overflow positions.**
- 166 overflows have two positions that disagree with neither by the named water. The place rule settled 13.
- Some ids join two assets (Holy Island STW: "Birkley Burn" live, "NORTH SEA" in the return).
- An authoritative third source: the EA's *Consented Discharges to Controlled Waters with Conditions* (OGL). It has the outlet grid reference, receiving water and permit number. Check its licence and fields, then use it to settle conflicts and to find each outfall's water.
- The bathing water features file (`environment.data.gov.uk/doc/bathing-water/<id>/features.json`) gives the EA's own overflow points near each bathing water.

**C. Checked joins (`data/raw/overflow_joins.csv`, 6 rows).** Each was settled by hand from OS OpenMap Local (OGL), the stream lines OS Open Rivers leaves out.
- To do the same at scale:
  - Download the OS OpenMap Local tiles (about 50–150 MB each).
  - For every counted overflow snapped 200 m or more onto a link not named like its own water, trace the OpenMap Local line from the outfall to where it meets a drawn river.
  - Culverts break these lines, so stop where they break and flag the case rather than guess.
- Cases known and not yet joined:
  - Stainforth WwTW: 61.5 spills a year, placed on the Fylde in both datasets, though its water body is in Ribblesdale. Edisford Bridge misses it.
  - Three Caldy Brook overflows at Chester, snapped onto the Shropshire Union Canal.
  - Benson STW, Sudbury WRC, Bailey and Coates CSO, and Ilkley Bridge Lane. Per #181, expect no accuracy gain; fix them for the map.
- Audit scripts from 11 Oct are in the private repo, `audit-2026-10-11/`:
  - `snap_audit.py`, `route_consistency.py`, `oml_mouths.py`, `oml_box.py`, `position_rule.py`, `place_rule.py`;
  - they are rough, and paths are passed as arguments.

**D. Lake model.**
- Lakes on the way (#176) use the spot's own lake constants: 0.05 m/s drift, and area dilution 1 + A/5 km². Neither constant is fitted. Residence time and depth are not modelled, so Grasmere still passes 58% of a spill to Rydal Water. The reviewer says a spill has dissipated by Rydal's outflow.
- The near-field change (section 3) has about 16 bad lake samples to be tested against.
- Wind decides where river water goes at Waterhead. The outside reviewer's local group has testing data there; Ethan is asking for it. Do not build a wind term before that data arrives.
- Lake distances are straight lines, some over land: Pooley Bridge has 6.7 of 10.9 km outside the lake.

**E. Side-arm head rule (#179).**
- It applies only to spots that name their river. Map clicks keep the old rule, so a click on Queen Elizabeth Gardens still traces the Nadder.
- Moving `anypoint.js` to the same rule needs each link's upstream neighbours in the click index.

**F. Feed freshness (#180).**
- The per-record rule needs at least 95% of a company's records stamped within a day. Northumbrian (1.4%) and Southern (0.3%) stamp only on change, so their frozen records cannot be told apart.
- Click-anywhere does not age readings.
- Past scored rows were not recomputed: about 250 of 48,090.
- WSX10000 and WSX10122 sit 41 m apart on Halse Water and may be one outfall listed twice. Wessex's map or the permit register would settle it.

**G. Accuracy story (#178, #182).**
- The spot level that sends alerts is never scored; one overflow's chance is. Design a spot-level score and add it to `docs/PREREGISTRATION-2027.md` as a dated amendment before 1 May 2027.
- The per-spot score boxes are near-empty: Henley's one spill was 47 km away.
- Re-run `scripts/live_recalibration_test.py` with the same rule each month. Thames and Southern Water forecast about ten times their observed rate this autumn.
- The High line has caught few spills outside United Utilities: 15 of 638.

## 5. Ground rules learned this round

- **State every model test before running it**, and commit and push the statement first. The urban, junction, named-snap and recalibration ideas all looked right, and all failed. A fix that feels obviously right still needs its test.
- **One heavy evaluation at a time on the 16 GB Mac.** A full chain peaks at about 4 GB.
- **Never copy OpenStreetMap positions into `spots.csv` or the joins file** (LICENSE-DATA.md). OpenStreetMap may be used to check; record OGL or CC0 sources.
- **Do not name members of the public who wrote in privately**, in this repo or in PRs.
- **When a site check is ambiguous at map precision, count the overflow.** An uncounted overflow that reaches a spot is the worse error. Say in the joins file why.
