# Review, 12 November 2026

A critical review of the swimmer's site and of how the research reaches it. Built from the live data issued
12 November 10:35 UTC, served locally from this commit's pages. "Measured" means I ran it. "Inferred" means I did not.

## 1. The short version

The forecast is honest and the engineering is careful. The product is hard to use because it says too much, and the
research is hard to find because it was moved out of the navigation and its own records have fallen behind.

Fixed in this PR: the home page's load jump, its repeated counts, its desktop grid, the no-photo spot header and one
false sentence about water temperature (`docs/DESIGN.md`, "The home page, tightened").

Not fixed here, in order of value:
1. The spot page is too long (section 3).
2. Research records say four live features are not live (section 4).
3. A first visit still shows a national tally before any place near the visitor (section 2).
4. The accuracy story is the weak point of every pitch and is two clicks from where a swimmer decides (section 5).

Still open:
- **No place of the visitor's own on a first visit.** The page opens on 133 spots as a tally. The location pin is an
  icon inside the search field. A plain button, "Spots near me", under the search would give a first-time visitor
  three real rows. It was a button until 29 October and was folded into the pin to save a row. With the hint gone
  there is room for it again. This is a design choice for Ethan, so it is not built.
- **The strip's "67 low risk" is a national count.** It means little to someone who swims in one river. With a
  location or saved spots it could count those instead. Inferred, not tested with users.
- **Thirteen closed area folds.** Two taps from any forecast that is not very high. Opening the visitor's nearest
  area by default needs the location above.

## 3. The spot page

Measured on River Severn in Shrewsbury at 1,440 px: 5,157 px tall, 1,744 words, 18 folds. On 11 November the
Dartington page measured 6,033 px and 1,506 words at phone width.

- **The answer is clear. Everything after it competes.** Five days, live status, where the risk comes from, what is
  not known, four condition tiles, forecast for today, latest readings, evidence and history, a guide, reviews,
  visit notes, nearby spots, actions, technical details. Each was added for a reason. Together they read as a
  report.
- **The sentence over the five days can run to nine lines on a phone** (Shrewsbury: a poor classification, recent
  spills and a Welsh gap in one paragraph). The day rows, which are the answer to "when", start below the first
  screen. The order is pinned by a test, so I left it. Suggested: cap the sentence at its first two clauses and put
  the rest behind "Why".
- **Two openings.** A spot with a photo reads down the left edge. A spot without one (25 of 133, and every point
  clicked on the map) is centred on a phone. Both were chosen on purpose at different times. One of them should win.
  Not changed here because it reverses a choice Ethan made.
- **Suggested shape:** answer, five days, one "why" tile, conditions. Then a single "More about this place" fold
  holding evidence, guide, reviews and notes. That would cut the page roughly in half. Inferred from the section
  heights, not built.

## 4. Research integration

Established by reading the built site and the source (file references in the PR description).

- **Research has no entry in the header or the phone's More menu.** This was decided on 6 November ("one product in
  the bar"). The only route from the home page is one footer link, about 2,600 px down on a phone. The Lab, the model
  scorecard and the catalogue are good work that almost no visitor will find.
- **The catalogue is behind the site.** `data/research/research_catalogue.v1.json` still describes the lake E. coli
  range (#585), the winter water temperature (#638) and the river-rise history (#631) as waiting to merge. All three
  are merged and live on spot pages. `missing-event-end` is "scheduled" for a re-measure on or after 9 November.
  The file's `written` date is 31 October. A visiting scientist reading the catalogue is told less than a swimmer.
- **The method page contradicts itself on lakes.** `methods.html` says lakes have a temperature estimate all year
  since 31 October, and two paragraphs later says "Lakes have no estimate". Not edited here: it is scientific copy.
- **The Accuracy page does not link the model scorecard** (`models.html`), which is the page that says which tests
  are running and when each reads out.
- **Three footers** with 12, 7 and 4 links (app, prose pages, Lab). The Lab's has no Accuracy or Method.
- **Orphans:** `quality.html` (one inbound link, from About), `data-feed.html` (one, from For organisations),
  `sampling-planner.html` (Research and the Lab's sampling exhibit only). Waterhead's samples feed the Windermere
  evidence timeline but no Windermere spot links the Waterhead page.
- **What works:** research that passed its test is shown where the swimmer is, with its error stated (water
  temperature, lake E. coli range, river-rise history). That is the right pattern. The gap is finding the rest.

Suggested, smallest first:
1. Bring the catalogue and register up to date and add a test that fails when an entry says "once merged" for a
   merged PR.
2. Link the scorecard from the Accuracy page, and Waterhead from the Windermere spots.
3. One footer for all three page kinds.
4. One line on the home page under the list: what is being tested now and when it reads out, from the scorecard.
   This puts research on the first page without putting it back in the bar. Ethan's call.

## 5. Product

- **Accuracy.** Live, 29 September to 10 November: 35% of spills had a High risk warning, and 49% of warnings were
  followed by a spill (Accuracy page). By company it runs from 8% (Anglian) to 47% (Southern). The site says this
  plainly, which is to its credit. But a swimmer deciding at a spot sees a level and no hit rate. One line under the
  level ("warnings here came true about 1 time in 2") would be more honest than the level alone. The per-spot scores
  exist (`spot_scores.json`).
- **Too many surfaces.** Plan, Saved, lists, outings, organisers, sites, signs, records, embed, feeds, Lab, five
  research viewers. The 6 November strategy review already said the site should read as one thing. Each surface
  costs a test suite and a paragraph of copy. Nothing should be added to the swimmer's site until something is
  removed or folded.
- **Wording is exact and heavy.** "No monitored overflow found upstream", "under official advice restricting
  swimming". These are correct. They are also the longest strings on every screen. A comprehension session
  (planned, not run) would show which ones people read.
- **Winter.** Bathing-water advice and E. coli estimates are out of season. The site's most distinctive live value
  until May is spills upstream of saved spots, which is the feed on the home page. That argues for making saving a
  spot the main action of a first visit.
