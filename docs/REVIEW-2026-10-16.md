## 2. The site and its interface

Checked in the desktop browser pane at about 800 px and at 375 × 812 (phone emulation), on the
live site: home (list and map), a lake spot (Waterhead), a very-high river spot (Greenholme), a
map click (Laughern Brook), Plan a swim, Saved, Accuracy, Method, Coasts. All 349 site tests
pass locally (`node --test tests/*.cjs`).

### What works

- The design system holds: one typeface pair, one radius, no pills or shadows, the paper and
  slate palette, the level colours only where they mean something. Pages read as one product.
- The headline answers the question first ("Low risk / For the next five days"; "Very high risk /
  Sewage spills right now / Low risk tomorrow"), then says what the level counts and what it does
  not, then the action. That is the right order and the wording is honest.
- The list's answer line ("Tomorrow: 67 of 105 spots at low risk · Show") and the area grouping
  make the list scannable. The five-day strip per row works at phone width.
- Click anywhere is fast, names the watercourse, says "Unlisted point: not hand-checked", and
  where no overflow is within 60 km shows rain in the days instead of a blank.
- Caveats are placed, not repeated.

### What could be better (verified)

1. **Spot pages are long and mostly empty below the fold.** On a phone the Waterhead page runs:
   headline, scope paragraph, flood-alert note, issued time, five-day table, "not a water test",
   access note, nearby, Practical guide (empty), Recent visits (empty), Swimmers' reviews (empty),
   seven tiles, service record, overflows, day-by-day, share, log, tell Ethan, technical details.
   The reviews index on the live site is empty (`reviews/index.json`: `"spots":{}, "visits":{}`),
   no spot has a guide photo, and 60 of 105 have no guide. So three community sections draw
   three empty tiles on nearly every page. (guide.js:138–141, visits.js:192–203, reviews.js:173.)
2. **"High risk: rated poor" dominates the list.** 13 of the 16 "high or very high" rows on 16 Oct
   are bathing waters whose 2025 rating sets the level all year (levels.js:197–207, 412). The
   forecast for those days is hidden behind the rating. A swimmer at Ilkley cannot see whether
   today is a good or bad day by the forecast's own reckoning without opening the page.
3. **"No monitored overflow found upstream" is a quarter of the list** (24 of 105) and reads
   as a level in the list and on the map (teal). The rows carry rain, nothing else. These spots
   have the least to say and the same visual weight as a forecast.
4. **The 6 MB overflow layer** (`data/overflows.geojson`, 821 kB gzipped) loads on any desktop
   or full-screen map at zoom 9 or more (index.html:2076–2085) and on the spot page in the
   desktop layout. The phone's small map is spared. A per-tile or per-area split would cut
   it to tens of kB per view; the click-anywhere squares already do this for their own data.
5. **"Flood alerts not checked"** appeared on 48 of 105 spot pages in the 21:03 build because the
   EA did not answer. The build asks the national flood list first and gives up for the rest.
   A spot page then shows a bold warning about a check that failed, which reads like a hazard.
6. **The Accuracy page is complete and honest, and very dense.** It is 14 sections. The four
   figures that matter (warned of 20% of spills; 39% of warnings true; 93 in 100 ranking; live
   error 27% lower) sit under two paragraphs and a table. A one-screen summary with a "by
   company" warning (the model was trained at one of nine companies) belongs at the top.
7. **Plan a swim starts empty.** It asks for a place before showing anything. The home page
   already has "Show spots near me"; the plan page could show the nearest spots first and let
   the person change the start.
8. **Saved page empty state** is a tile that says "No saved spots yet" and a second tile about the
   swim journal. For a first visitor it is two paragraphs about things they have not done.
9. **The map click's rain-only days show "–"** for the level and a thin rain bar. The bar has no
   scale and no number, where the list rows say "29 mm of rain in 48 h".
10. **Two 404s in the console on every spot page** were my own typed addresses (`spot.html?id=`
    and `accuracy.html`), not the site's. No console errors from the site itself.

### What could be better (inferred, not verified here)

- Screenshots taken within three seconds of navigation show the page at low opacity, in the
  pane's hidden tab. The CSS has a 0.22 s rise animation (index.html:111–113) and a 1.2 s
  skeleton breathe; a hidden tab pauses animations, so this is most likely the pane, not the
  site. The 5 Oct LCP work found 4.3 s on a phone; a real-device check is still worth doing.
- The home list is 105 rows in one scroll. Usage data (11 Oct) showed near-zero visits, so no
  evidence yet on whether people use the list, the map or search first.

## 3. The pipeline and the predictions

An agent read the model, ingest, network, scoring and build code and ran the Python suite (741
passed, 78 s). I re-read the two findings that matter most against the files named. Nothing was
run beyond the tests.

**Verdict.** The plumbing is defensive and honest: a failed feed, a short read, a frozen record,
missing rain and stale state each have a rule, a build warning and usually a test. The service
record on the Accuracy page bears this out (29 of 29 mornings with a forecast; 133 of 133 builds
since 4 Oct published; one feed outage in 289 polls). The weak layer is the model. Its fitted
parts were fitted on one company and one year, one of them for a different model than the one
in production; its physical parts are constants nobody has fitted; and the number a swimmer acts
on, the spot level, is still not scored.

### Findings, ranked

1. **The lead calibration was fitted for a model that is no longer in production.** Verified:
   `data/processed/lead_calibration.json` says `model: "United Utilities 2023-2024; verified on
   2025", fitted_on: 2025`; `data/processed/spill_model.pkl` says `trained_on = "United Utilities
   2023-2025 (refit on all years…)"`; `forecast.py` applies the first to the second. The isotonic
   map corrects a 2023–24 model's over-prediction of 2025; the refit model already saw 2025. At
   lead 0 the map is near identity (a −0.02, b 1.02), so the effect is at the longer leads.
   Inferred: a plausible contributor to the live over-forecasting (0.4–0.5 bin verifies at 28%;
   Thames and Southern forecast six to eight times their observed rate), which the recalibration
   test of 11 Oct tried to patch downstream and failed.
2. **The length dilution is computed on the 60 km trace, not the catchment.** Verified:
   `transport.py:942` gets `lup` from `upstream_lengths(net, pin.trace_node, cap)`; lines 1055–1056
   use it for both the overflow and the spot. The full `net.upstream_m` (`spot_up_full`) is used
   only by the rejected `flow` and `urban` modes. The README and the module docstring call the
   denominator "the total upstream network length", which it is not. On a big river the
   denominator is the network within 60 km path distance, so weights are larger than the stated
   formula gives. Line 1055 also carries a dead term, `… * ov["link_length"] * 0`. This is a
   different candidate from the three dilution replacements that failed (flow, urban, junction):
   same proxy, uncapped denominator.
3. **Missing rain days count as dry in the rolling features.** Verified: `features.py:63–71`
   uses `rolling(..., min_periods=1).sum()` for the 3, 7 and 30-day totals and `0.0 if isnan` for
   the antecedent index; only the day's own rain gates the row (`forecast.py:349`). A gap in the
   past days, or the 24-hour stale-cache path in `rainfall.py`, lowers the long features without
   a mark. The stated rule is "missing rainfall is unknown, not dry". No test covers a NaN day.
4. **A forecast built on a day-old rain run carries no per-spot mark.** Verified: `fetch_forecast`
   serves cached frames up to 24 h; `forecast_point` never reads the frame's issue time; the
   stale count reaches only `build.warnings`. The page's stale notice keys on the build time.
5. **The spot level is not scored, and the Accuracy page does not say so.** Verified: the live
   scorer scores `forecast_overflows` only; `forecast_points` is logged, never scored; the
   pre-registered fix (PREREGISTRATION §17, Amendment 1, S1/S2) is written, and `travel_h` is
   now logged for it, but no code implements it. Its data file is due 31 Jan 2027.
6. **Transport and level constants are guesses, and seven rejected modes stay as code paths.**
   Verified: 0.5 m/s river, 0.05 m/s lake, T90 30 h, 48 h recent, 60 km cap (`config.py:97–102`);
   L0 5 km, A0 5 km², 0.7 low-confidence factor (`transport.py:93–99`); level cuts 0.15/0.40/0.70
   in three places. `upstream_overflows` accepts 48 combinations of options; one is in production;
   `flow`, `urban`, `catchment`, `lake`, `river`, `residence`, `line` all failed or were superseded.
7. **Evaluations cannot be reproduced from a commit alone.** Verified: every evaluation reads
   `state/overflows.parquet`, rebuilt from the uncommitted live snapshot; the preregistration
   hashes the scripts and the EDM events but not the overflow table.
8. **GitHub's schedule fires a fifth of the time.** Verified from the live service record: 289
   runs in 29 days against 1,392 asked (20.8%); median 7 a day, median gap 1.98 h, longest gap
   7.7 h. The scoring rule drops days whose longest gap exceeds 8 h (8 and 9 Oct are missing).
   Pushes to main made 75 of the last 133 builds. The runs depend on how often someone merges.
9. **Training and inference use different day boundaries.** Verified: `scripts/train.py:94`
   floors on UTC; `forecast.py:343` converts to Europe/London first. An hour's offset on every
   rain feature in summer. Low impact, untested.
10. **The forecast log only grows.** Verified: 2.6 million scored rows, about 53,000 a build day;
    the release guard refuses a smaller file. About 1–1.5 GB a year against a 2 GB asset limit.
11. **Silent fallbacks.** Verified: a missing `spill_model.pkl` falls back to climatology with a
    log line only; EA level and flood lookups time out routinely (48 of 105 spots unchecked in
    one build) and are reported, not fatal. Cache eviction between release publishes would lose
    up to 12 h of log; the scorer sees a gap, not wrong numbers.
12. **Duplication.** `Europe/London` is defined in nine files; the coverage file, poll log and
    slots-per-day constants in two; the 6-hour feed-current rule hard-coded beside its constant;
    two spill-day exploders; a Welsh test result hard-coded as data in `build_site.py:225`.
13. **Determinism and cost are fine.** Snap, outlet, dead-end and cycle orders are sorted and
    tested (PR #233). About 15–20 Open-Meteo requests a build against 10,000 a day; 6–11 minutes
    a build against a 25-minute timeout; the network pickle is 113 MB on disk, about 900 MB in
    memory.

## 4. Data not yet used

An agent fetched the licence and catalogue pages for about twenty candidates (its full table is
in the PR description's source list; the ranked ones are here). I re-read the catalogue record
for the first one. "Verified" means the page or endpoint was fetched; "inferred" is marked.

**The one that matters most is already half-known to the project.** The Environment Agency's
*Event Duration Monitoring - Storm Overflow - Start/Stop Detailed Data*
(environment.data.gov.uk/dataset/e9677ac1-fd32-4ceb-88a0-2735be5f27c7) holds a row per
discharge event, start and stop, for **all ten companies that operate in England, for 2024 and
2025**, plus 2026 year-to-date for Thames Water and Welsh Water. Verified on 16 Oct: 23 files
listed in the catalogue, revised 15–16 Oct 2026; the platform page gives the licence as Open
Government Licence v3 (`docs/WALES-PLAN-2026-10.md` §2.2 settled this on 4 Oct; the catalogue's
`license_id` is still empty). The repo already reads the three Welsh Water workbooks
(`scripts/import_ea_welsh_water.py`, `edm_events.read_ea_dcww_events`) as a Welsh test set.
**Nothing reads the other nine companies' files.** The spill model and its per-overflow layer are
fitted on United Utilities alone, and the Accuracy page's by-company table shows what that
costs: 402 of 425 true warnings at one company; Thames and Southern forecasting six to eight
times their observed rate. Two years of per-event truth for every company is the data that
fixes this. Caveats: the files' layouts differ (CSV and XLSX, Excel dates and ISO text; the
Welsh reader handles both); event definitions may differ by company (the DSP technical note,
`Technical Detail DSP April 2026.docx`, is unopened); and the download route is a signed-link
request that the Welsh importer already implements off CI.

Ranked after it (publisher; licence as stated; verified unless marked):

1. **South West Water EDM Monthly** (Stream, ArcGIS; "licensed under CC BY 4.0"): 330,278 events
   Jan 2025–Jan 2026 with an `eventVerified` flag. South West Water has never been scorable from
   its live feed alone (review of 2 Oct).
2. **Northumbrian Water EDM Spill Data 2020–2024** (Stream; "Licensed under CC BY 4.0"): 471,962
   events, five years, for per-overflow priors and drift checks. The item sits on a personal
   account inside the hub (inferred: check it persists). Dates are `dd/mm/yyyy hh:mm` strings.
3. **Open-Meteo Ensemble API** (CC BY 4.0; same 10,000-a-day budget): MOGREPS-UK 2 km, 3 members,
   5 days; ICON-EU-EPS 40 members; ECMWF 51 members, 15 days. Gives the spill probability a band
   on days 3–5. Budget: the 105 spots use 332 rain cells (7 batched calls a build); the
   click-anywhere squares use 1,588 cells, too many for a per-member call on every build.
4. **Open-Meteo Flood API** (GloFAS v4, 5 km, daily discharge to 210 days with ensemble
   statistics): a forecast flow for the days ahead where today the dilution holds today's flow.
   "The closest river might not be selected correctly" at 5 km; needs a check against the EA
   gauges the site already reads. GloFAS's own terms page was not fetched.
5. **EA real-time rainfall API** (OGL v3; the flood-monitoring host the build already reads):
   1,043 gauges, 15-minute totals, a month's window, transferred "once or twice per day". An
   observed-rain check on the forecast-held "right now" rule, a day late.
6. **Catchment Data Explorer, reasons for not achieving good status** (OGL; one 16 MB CSV,
   quarterly): per water body, "agriculture and rural land management" and other pressures with
   certainty. Joins on the WFD water body id the annual returns already carry. A diffuse-pollution
   prior for the E. coli estimate, which is miscalibrated in Wales, and a sentence on the spot page.
7. **Thames Water Open Data API** (terms read: a CC BY-style licence, unnamed; key requirement
   not stated): discharge alerts back to April 2022 per a developer's write-up (inferred). A
   backfill before the EA file starts.
8. **Citizen E. coli** (Water Rangers AQuA Sheffield, ODC-By, 83 points; UKCEH Wharfe 2019–21,
   OGL): small held-out checks at non-bathing sites. Never training.
9. **UKCEH Land Cover Map 2023, 1 km** (EIDC licence: "Personal Use", "may not make the Data
   available on any internet site"): a catchment land-use feature could be computed offline and
   only derived scores published; needs a licence email.

Ruled out, with the reason: EA Hydrology blue-green algae sensors (105 measures, zero readings
in 2026); Met Office DataHub (key, paid ensembles, UKV already via Open-Meteo); CEDA radar
(login); Earthwatch WaterBlitz, SAS Data HQ, Big River Watch (no licence or no E. coli); the
Hydrological Outlook (monthly PDF); ECMWF open data (redundant with Open-Meteo); Copernicus lake
water quality (turbidity and trophic index only at 100 m, registration likely). Still waiting:
Dŵr Cymru's live layer (no licence), UKCEH Bloomin' Algae (spreadsheet on request, no licence
stated), SEPA (pointed to Scottish Water).

Acceptance: a test asserts `lead_calibration["model"] == SpillModel.load().trained_on`; the
Method page's "Skill with real forecasts" paragraph says what was done; a pre-stated note names
the live `by_rain` and `reliability` bins expected to move over the next four weeks.

Why: finding 3.2. The stated formula uses total upstream length; the code uses the 60 km trace.
Do: add `DIPCAST_DILUTION_MODE=length_full` in `transport.py` using `spot_up_full` for the spot
and `net.upstream_m` at the overflow's node; delete the dead `* 0` term at line 1055. Write
`docs/DILUTION-FULL-LENGTH-2026-10.md` with the conditions (the pattern in
`docs/SPILL-SIZE-TEST-2026-10.md`: leave-one-year-out, leave-one-site-out, forward, placebo

Acceptance: the doc's conditions were pushed before the run (commit times in the PR); the
default changes only if every condition holds; the README's Method paragraph matches the code
either way.

### Bugs and inconsistencies (verified unless marked)

1. **"Today" means the build's day, not the reader's.** `levels.js:23` (`today()`) and
   `index.html:1181` (`dateLabel`) key every day label to `serverToday`, the build date. Nothing
   compares it with the device's date. The stale notice fires only after 8 h (`STALE_MIN`). The
   builds come about seven times a day with gaps up to 7.7 h (section 3.8), so between midnight
   and the next build a reader sees yesterday's row labelled "Today", "Tomorrow" meaning today,
   and the answer line "Tomorrow: 67 of 105 spots at low risk" about the wrong day. Alerts and
   the organisers' date picker use the same words. B1 narrows the window; the page should also
   relabel by the device's date and say "issued yesterday at 23:24".
2. **Overflows of zero weight are counted as if they mattered.** Twelve contributor rows carry
   `weight: 0.0` (rounded) at Windermere (Lakeside, Fellfoot, Millerground, Rayrigg, Waterhead)
   and Ullswater (Pooley Bridge). Pooley Bridge's one overflow, Glenridding works, is 11.5 km
   through the lake at 64 h travel, weight 0.000, so its `sum_weight` is 0.0 and the headline
   reads "Low risk for the next five days" from a model that cannot read anything else there.
   Waterhead says "Under one spill is expected from the 4 overflows upstream", counting Grasmere
   works at weight 0.000. The honest words are nearer the no-overflow label: "the one monitored
   overflow upstream is too far through the lake to count". Ethan's call on the wording.
3. **Placeholder receiving-water names reach the map.** `overflows.geojson` has 38 overflows with
   `receiving_watercourse` "#N/A", 7 "N/A", 1 "None", 1 "0", and 257 with an empty site name. The
   empty names are handled ("no name in its feed"); the placeholders are printed as water names
   in the map pop-up (`index.html` `loadOverflows`) and in the spot's overflow list where that
   field shows. `overflow_names.py` has no rule for them.
4. **The answer line ignores the picked day.** Choosing "Mon 19 Oct" updates the list ("On
   Monday", 59 low) and the map, but the line above still says "Tomorrow: 67 of 105 spots at low
   risk". Ethan chose "weekend day" for that line (PR #216); with a day picked it should follow it.
5. **Overflows joined at the spot read "0.0 km, 0 h".** Culham STW at Clifton Hampden and Ilkley
   Middleton CSO at Cromwheel have `distance_km: 0.0, travel_h: 0.0` (joins settled in October).
   Inferred, not checked on the page: the overflow list shows them as "0 h away", where "at the
   spot" would be the right words.
6. **Small numbers worth knowing:** 12 of 14,631 overflows were discharging at 23:24 on 16 Oct
   (a dry spell); 1 has been discharging since 28 Sep (SWS00007, Southern); 12 contributor rows at
   listed spots have no long-term spill count (filled from company medians, PR #180); 1 Wessex
   record at French Weir has not updated since 31 Mar 2026 (known, "no update since"). Everything
   else scanned was in range: 510 of 510 day rows `ok`, all risks and probabilities within [0, 1],
   all day rows consecutive from the build date, all weather rows aligned, no gauge over 15 km,
   no level outside its typical range with the wrong label, no duplicate contributor.

   place field. No console errors on any page visited.

### Features worth building (ordered by what they would do for a swimmer or a group)

Each is grounded in data the build already has, unless marked.

- **The upstream trace on the map.** For the open spot, draw the river path the forecast traced
  (the 60 km of network upstream) as a line, with the counted overflows on it. The build has the
  trace; a simplified polyline per spot is 10–50 kB. It answers the question every reviewer has
  asked ("why does an overflow 40 km away count?") in one picture, and it would have shown the
  Loughrigg Tarn fault to anyone who looked. Medium.
- **"When will it clear?"** The now-risk already carries `clears_at` and `clears_by` ("die-off").
  Say it in words on the page and in the alert: "The spill upstream should have passed and died
  off by Saturday 22:47." Then extend it to rain: after a wet day, the five-day table shows the
  recovery, but no sentence names the first clear day. Small.
- **A spot's last 30 days.** One strip per spot: the rain, the days its overflows actually spilled
  (from the live history the scorer uses), and what the forecast said. Trust is built by letting
  a swimmer see the record at their own spot, not on the Accuracy page. `spot_scores.json` has
  the counts; the days are in the forecast log. Medium.
- **"How was it when I swam?"** The journal keeps the day and the forecast shown. On the next
  visit, add what the overflows did that day (observed spill or not) beside the entry. Same data
  as the strip above. Small once the strip exists.
- **Two more alert kinds.** (a) All clear: a saved spot back at low after high. (b) A spill has
  started upstream of a saved spot, with the arrival time from `travel_h`: "Grasmere works is
  discharging; its water reaches Waterhead in about 30 hours." SAS alerts say a spill happened;
  nobody says when it arrives. Worker change (Ethan deploys); the build's cadence (B1) limits it.
  Small to medium.
- **Static feeds.** `alerts.json` already has every spot's level, headline and action. Writing
  `feed.xml` per spot and per area at build (Atom) costs nothing and plugs into Slack's RSS app,
  Discord bots, Feedly and IFTTT, which is how a club would pipe alerts into its WhatsApp group.
  Small.
- **A morning post per area.** From the same file, a Worker or Action posts to Mastodon and
  Bluesky at 07:30: "Yorkshire today: 7 of 13 spots low; Wharfe at Burley very high." Needs the
  accounts (Ethan) and the words (Ethan). Small; a growth channel that costs nothing to run.
- **A river stretch, not a point.** Paddlers and rowers put in at one place and take out at
  another. Two clicks on the map, and the page gives the worst level along the stretch and where
  it is, from the click-anywhere squares. Nothing else does this. Medium to large; needs the
  path between two links, which the tiles' upstream lists can give for a same-river stretch.
- **Postcode search.** The Plan page knows 26,000 places (OS Open Names) but not postcodes.
  postcodes.io (OGL, free, CORS) turns a postcode into a point in the browser, for the many who
  refuse location on desktop. Small; one more third-party host for the privacy notice.
- **Forecast confidence.** With ensemble rain (D4), days 3–5 can say "uncertain: the rain
  forecasts disagree" where the members' spread is wide. Depends on D4.
- **A season report per spot.** Each spring (the annual returns land about 30 March): spill days
  observed, warnings issued and how many came true, weekend days at low risk, the wettest week.
  A page per spot and one for England; the press hook SwimSignal does not yet have. Medium;
  first possible spring 2027 with six months of log.
- **A data-quality page.** The 125 open overflow position conflicts (docs/OVERFLOW-POSITIONS) and
  the placeholder names above, published as a list that invites corrections from the companies
  and the Agency. Civic value, and it shows the method's honesty. Small.
