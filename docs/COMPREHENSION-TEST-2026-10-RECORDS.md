## 2. Analysis of 8 to 10 records

Count people, not answers. Fill this table from the records, one count per line. The rows follow the

results cannot move them.

Apply D1 to D8 and A1 to A7 as written; they were set before any session. There is no per-person
overall score (D1): the tally counts the measures. Never pick the reading that passes.

A participant who did not reach a task (out of time, call dropped) is left out of that task's "out of",
and the table says how many were left out.

Repeat use (D4): a return counts only if they confirm it and name what they used it for (plan, section
7). The denominator is everyone who completed a session, fixed before the first follow-up; never reduce
it after the answers are in. Someone who did not agree to follow-ups, or did not reply, is unknown use,
not a return. Report, beside the count: how many completed a session, how many agreed to follow-ups,
how many answered at 7 days and at 14 days, and how many were lost along the way.

### 2.2 What counts as a problem to fix

Four kinds, each from the plan:

1. **A fail line crossed** (table above). The plan's section 8 already says what follows each one:
   task 2 first changes the answer's words, task 3 the missing-information grouping, task 1 a look at
   where people went instead, task 4 the save and return route, task 5 briefings by hand. Do not choose
   a different response after seeing the results.
2. **Any one clear case of reading the page as a safety guarantee, or as "water tested"**, whatever
   the count. The plan marks "tested" as a failure of the forecast-versus-test message; D5 adds what
   follows: a wording fix and a focused retest before the site is promoted more widely. Routine bug
   fixes may continue meanwhile.
3. **The same misreading by two or more people**, even under a fail line. The plan: "A failure by two
   people is still worth fixing."
4. **Everything else** goes in the list with its severity (blocks, slows, cosmetic; plan, section 6)
   and the number of people, and waits. A single person's cosmetic friction is a note, not a task.

The plan also says what the results cannot show: eight people give direction, not proof, and a pass by
eight does not show the wording works for everyone. Write that in the summary.

A broad visual redesign follows only if a task fails for a reason a specific design change fixes (plan,
section 8).

### 2.3 Keep quotes apart from interpretation

- The record has separate fields: "Said" for the participant's words, in quotation marks, and
  "Moderator's reading" for what you think they meant. Keep them separate in the summary too.
- Score task 2 from the quoted words alone, before you look at the other tasks. If someone else took
  notes, they score the same quotes without seeing your score; list any disagreement and settle it by the
  rubric's wording, not by memory of the session.
- A finding is written in two parts: what was observed (count, quotes, P-codes) and what it suggests.
  An invented example, not a result: "4 of 9 named no gap on Coniston Water (P02 'it all looks
  covered', P05, P07, P08)" and, separately, "The EA advice link may read as a feature, not a gap."
- Do not paraphrase a quote to make it fit a category. If it does not fit, put it under "unclear" with
  the quote.
- Quote in the public summary only those who agreed (consent line 3), and only a quote that cannot
  identify them. Never use names, and drop any detail that could identify someone (a club, a place they
  live, a job, an unusual turn of phrase). Section 3's check applies to every quote.

## 3. The public summary

After the round, one public doc or issue holds only (plan, section 6):

- the tally table (section 2.1), with the wording version each participant saw;
- for repeat use, how many completed a session, agreed to follow-ups and answered (D4);
- the problems to fix, ranked as in section 2.2, each with its count and severity;
- quotes from those who agreed, and only those that pass the check below;
- the decisions taken under the plan's section 8;
- what was not tested (for example no overflow without a live feed in the Lake District).

Publish only genuinely anonymised findings. Before publishing, check every finding and quote against the
small sample: could someone who knows a participant recognise them from a quote, a detail, the session
date, their device, or the answers grouped under one code? If so, remove the detail, or drop the
participant codes from the public version. Publish nothing that fails this check. Coded notes are not
anonymous and are never published.

If a participant withdraws, delete their record, any recording and their contact details, and remove or
correct anything already published that could identify them.

Then delete the raw records and their consent answers, unless a participant agreed to more (plan,
section 6), and by 31 January 2027 at the latest. Contact details go sooner: when the last follow-up is
answered, or 21 days after the session, whichever comes first (D6). See the run sheet, section 6.
