Privacy notice

What SwimSignal collects, and what it does not. Last updated 4 October 2026.

Who is responsible

SwimSignal is run by one person, Ethan Buckley (ethanbuckley on GitHub), who is the controller for the personal data described here.

For anything private, including a data-protection request or a complaint, email hello@swimsignal.co.uk. A complaint is acknowledged within 30 days. For general questions you can also open an issue on the project's GitHub repository; issues are public, so do not put anything personal in one.

The short version

  • No accounts, no cookies and no advertising. There is no analytics script and no third-party tracking.
  • Your saved spots, your swim journal and your location stay on your device.
  • Opening the site sends your IP address to the services that supply its files, as it would for any website. They are listed below.

Who sees your IP address

When you open the site, your browser fetches files from these services, and each one receives your IP address and basic details of your browser:

  • GitHub, which hosts the site (GitHub Pages). GitHub logs these requests for its own security purposes and is responsible for those logs. Its privacy statement applies.
  • The OpenStreetMap Foundation, for the map images. Its privacy policy applies.

One more service is asked only when you choose to, on the Coverage page or on a bathing water's page: the data service at environment.data.gov.uk. Opening the list of Welsh bathing waters on the Coverage page asks it for Natural Resources Wales's current samples and forecasts. Opening "Today's EA advice", under an English bathing water on the Coverage page or on a bathing water's page, loads the Environment Agency's own panel for that site from it; the page loads the panel with scripts switched off and does not tell it which page you were on. Each time, the service receives your IP address and basic details of your browser. SwimSignal sends it nothing else. Natural Resources Wales's privacy notice and the Environment Agency's personal information charter apply.

SwimSignal asks for these files because the page cannot show its map or run without them. That is the legitimate interest these requests rest on. SwimSignal itself receives no personal data from them and keeps no logs. These services may handle your IP address outside the UK, under their own safeguards.

Your location

The list of spots can be sorted by distance from you, and Plan a swim can start from where you are. Your browser asks for your location only if you choose "Near me" on the list or "Use my location" on Plan a swim. The page uses it to order the spots by distance, and on Plan a swim to leave out those too far away, and for nothing else: it is not sent to SwimSignal or to anyone else, it is not stored, and it is gone when you close the page. A plan's address says "here" in place of where you are, so a link to it does not hold your location. Neither choice moves the map, so the map images fetched from OpenStreetMap do not reveal it either.

A town, village or district you type on Plan a swim is looked up on your device, in a list of places the page fetches from this site; what you type is not sent anywhere. The place goes into the page's address after a #, which browsers do not send to the site, so a link you share holds it. The map then moves to it, so the map images fetched from OpenStreetMap are of that area.

Saved spots

If you press Save on a spot, its identifier is kept in your browser's local storage, so the spot shows on your Saved page and at the top of the list. It stays on your device: it is not sent to SwimSignal or to anyone else. Clearing this site's data in your browser removes it. On an iPhone, Safari and a Home Screen app keep separate lists. If you make more than one list of saved spots, the names you give them are kept the same way, with which spots are in each.

So that a saved spot's card can say what has changed since you last looked, your browser also keeps a note for each saved spot of what it last showed you: the spot's level, how many overflows upstream were discharging, its Environment Agency rating, which forecast that was and when you looked. The note is kept in your browser's local storage and sent nowhere. It is removed when you remove the spot, and clearing this site's data removes it too.

"Share this list" makes a link holding the list's name and the identifiers of its spots, after a # that browsers do not send to the site. "Share this plan", on Plan a swim, makes the same kind of link for the spots the plan shows as nothing flagged, with the plan's day; it never holds where the plan starts from. Anyone you send a link to can see the list's name and which spots are on it. Opening such a link shows the list without keeping it; it is kept in your browser only if you press "Add to my lists".

"Log a swim", on a spot's page, keeps a swim journal the same way: the spot, the day and time, what the forecast showed then, and any note, minutes in the water and photos you add, in your browser's storage on this device (the photos in its IndexedDB), sent nowhere. You can delete a swim, or all of them, on the Saved page; clearing this site's data removes them too. "Save a copy" makes a file of the journal on your device, and "Add swims from a copy" reads one there; SwimSignal receives neither. "Share as a picture" draws the forecast into an image on your device; nothing is uploaded until you choose where to send it.

Offline copy

After your first visit, the browser keeps a copy of the page, its icons, the map library and the latest forecast (using a service worker), so the site still opens without signal. The copy holds only what every visitor downloads, and nothing about you. You can turn it off, which also deletes it, under "Saved spots, offline use and your data" at the bottom of the home page. Clearing this site's data removes it too. If you turn it off, that choice is kept in your browser's local storage, so the copy is not made again; it is sent nowhere.

If you email

If you email the operator, your address and what you write are used only to reply and to deal with any request or complaint. The basis is legitimate interests, or a legal obligation for a data-protection request. They are kept only as long as that takes, then deleted, and are never shared or sold. Email to hello@swimsignal.co.uk is held by Apple, in the operator's iCloud Mail account, in the meantime.

Feedback

The feedback form prepares an email on your device. It does not submit or save what you type. Nothing is sent to the operator until you send the email yourself; the email handling described above then applies. The copy button puts your draft on your device’s clipboard. You can instead open a public issue on GitHub; GitHub’s privacy statement applies to it. Do not put personal information in a public issue.

Practical guides

If you email details or photos for a spot's practical guide, the email is handled as above, with one addition: what you send may be published on the spot's page. A detail is shown as "A swimmer's suggestion", with the day and the name or initials you asked to be credited as, or "a swimmer" if you did not say. A photo is published only if your email says you took it, that it may be published with your credit, and that anyone who can be recognised in it is happy to be online. Before it is published, the photo is shrunk and everything else in the file is removed, including where it was taken and the phone's details. The basis is your consent. You can withdraw it at any time by email, and the operator then takes the detail or photo off the site. Guides are part of SwimSignal's public source code on GitHub, so an earlier version stays in its history.

If alerts are added

A planned feature would send a notification to your phone or computer when a saved spot's forecast reaches high or very high risk. If it is added, this notice will be updated first. It would store only what is needed to send those notifications, never share or sell it, and you would be able to turn it off at any time, which deletes it.

The server version

SwimSignal's code can also run as a server that forecasts any point you click. No such server runs for this site. Whoever runs one receives the coordinates you click and your IP address, and keeps a log of the forecasts issued (the coordinates, the time and the forecast values, with no IP address or identifier) to score their accuracy.

Your rights

UK data protection law gives you rights over personal data about you, including to be told what is held, to have it corrected or deleted, and to object. SwimSignal holds none, as described above; ask GitHub about its logs. Details and photos swimmers send for a practical guide are published as described under Practical guides. You can complain to the operator by email (above) and to the Information Commissioner's Office.

The operator is an individual, not a company. This notice describes current practice in plain English. It was checked against UK data protection law and the ICO's guidance on 29 September 2026, but not by a lawyer.

SwimSignal is a free, non-commercial pollution risk forecast for river and lake swim spots in England, run by Ethan Buckley. A forecast, not a water test: check the signs at the water before you swim.
