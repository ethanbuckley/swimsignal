For clubs, centres and leaders

This page is for clubs, activity centres, Scouts and Guides leaders, schools and event organisers. It covers swimming, ghyll scrambling, gorge walking, paddling, sailing, windsurfing and rowing on rivers and lakes in England. SwimSignal forecasts the risk of sewage pollution at a spot for the next five days.

What it cannot do

  • It is a forecast, not a water test. Nothing on the site says the water is safe.
  • It misses most spills. Its first live scores ran from 29 September to 4 October 2026. At the high risk line, it warned of about one in five spills. About two in five of its warnings were followed by a spill. The accuracy page has the latest figures.
  • It sees monitored storm overflows only. It does not see farm runoff, livestock, wildlife, misconnected drains or a pollution incident.
  • The level is about pollution only. Cold water, currents and high water stay in your own risk assessment.
  • It covers rivers and lakes in England. Coasts are not forecast.

The same level, different exposure

A spot's level is the same for every activity. What differs is how wet people get. Sewage bacteria and viruses get in through the mouth and through cuts. A group that goes under is more exposed than one that stays dry.

Swimming
Full immersion. Swimmers swallow some water, and children swallow more.
Ghyll scrambling and gorge walking
Full immersion, in jumps, pools and slides. The groups are often children with an activity centre.
Kayaking, canoeing and paddleboarding
Mostly on the water. Falls, capsize drills and rolling put heads under.
Dinghy sailing and windsurfing
On lakes and reservoirs mostly. A capsize or a fall puts people in the water.
Rowing
Splashes from the oars. A fall in is rare.

Paddle UK's guidance makes the same point: "Some paddlers will be more immersed in the water such as freestyle paddlers or those carrying out capsize drills." SwimSignal does not set a different level for each activity. That judgement stays with you.

What the levels mean for a group in the water

There are four levels: low, moderate, high and very high risk. The table on the method page shows what sets each one. Each spot's page puts one line under its level, saying what to do. The lines are written for swimmers. For a group, read "swimming" as any activity that puts people in the water.

Moderate risk
Take more care: young children, older people and anyone with a weakened immune system may want a lower day or spot.
High risk
Better to choose a lower day or spot. If you do swim, try not to swallow any water.
Very high risk
Avoid swimming here: choose a lower day or spot. The page names the day, as in "Avoid swimming here tomorrow".

All the lines, and the health advice they rest on, are under what to do at each level.

Ghylls and upland becks

Many ghylls and upland becks have no monitored storm overflow upstream. Their level then reads "No sewage risk from monitored overflows". That does not mean the water is clean. Livestock, wildlife and runoff after rain still carry bacteria into becks. So the page keeps one line in view: "Other risks apply: algae, wildlife, runoff and bathers. Check the signs at the water." Its advice there is "After heavy rain, wait a couple of days before swimming if you can."

A launch point or a ghyll that is not listed

Tap the river or lake on the map. SwimSignal traces the overflows upstream of that point and gives its five days. It works for any river or lake in England on Ordnance Survey's river network. A tapped point has no E. coli estimate, since that needs the rain at a listed spot.

Paddle UK's two questions

Paddle UK's water quality guidance asks paddlers a few questions before they go out. A spot's page answers two of them.

"Has there been heavy rainfall or extended dry periods in recent days?"
The page gives the rain at the spot in the 48 hours to midday today. It names the wettest day ahead when rain is coming. The five days are forecast from the rain on the overflows upstream. Where nothing upstream is monitored, the five days show the rain itself. SwimSignal does not judge dry spells.
"Are there CSOs upstream of your paddling location?"
A CSO is a combined sewer overflow. The spot's page lists the monitored storm overflows upstream, and says how many are discharging now. The organisers' page lists every one as a table and a CSV file. The overflow history page gives each one's spills from 2021 to 2025.

The Scouts' rule

The Scouts' water safety guidance says: "No water based activity should proceed if there are any concerns around water contamination in the planned location." It asks leaders to consider where sewage discharges and overflows are, and recent rain.

SwimSignal can give you a reason for concern: a high or very high risk day, or an overflow upstream discharging now. It cannot clear one. A low risk day means only that nothing the site looks at flags it.

Tools for planning a session

The organisers' page
Pick a spot and a day. It gives that day's level within the five days, every overflow upstream as a table and a CSV file, and a checklist.
A sign to print
Each spot has one, with a QR code to its page. It shows no level, since a printed level goes out of date. The organisers' page links it.
A live sign for a screen
The forecast card, large, updating itself. For a clubhouse or a centre's reception. The organisers' page links it.
The embed card
One spot's forecast on your own web page. The terms allow it on a non-commercial website only, with the card shown whole. Ask first about any other use: email hello@swimsignal.co.uk.
A sites view
Your launch points in one table, each with its five days, on one printable page.
A decision record
One printable page for a site and a day, with the inputs, their ages and blank lines for your decision.
Overflow history
Each spot's overflows upstream, with their spills and spill hours each year from 2021 to 2025. Its spot page links it. The link here is the Wharfe at Ilkley's.

If you see pollution

Report it to the Environment Agency on 0800 80 70 60, at any hour. In Wales, call Natural Resources Wales on 0300 065 3000.

On the coast

Coasteering, sea kayaking and surfing are outside the forecast. The coverage page links the official results for each English beach.

Governing bodies

The RYA, the body for sailing and windsurfing, calls for "real-time informed choices about where and when to participate" in its water quality position. None of the bodies named on this page endorses SwimSignal.
