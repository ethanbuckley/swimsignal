Wessex Water's river samples

What these samples do not tell you

  • what the water is like now
  • what the water is like at the spot itself, where the sampling point is some way along the river from it
  • a forecast
  • a bathing water rating, which is worked out from many samples taken in the bathing season over four years, never from one
  • that the water is safe to swim
  • where the E. coli came from
  • how well SwimSignal's forecast did: these samples are not compared with it here

How they were taken

Wessex Water takes these samples from rivers in its region and has them analysed at an accredited laboratory. It says it uses them to understand the effect of its works and overflows on the rivers. They are not the Environment Agency's statutory bathing water samples, and they follow no bathing water rules: they were taken through the year, in any weather. Each count is E. coli per 100 ml as the company publishes it; a count given as "below" or "above" is the lab's limit, not a measurement. Times are as published, with no time zone given. The company's layer names no sampling site, so SwimSignal placed each point on the river map beside the spot it is linked from, and says how far along the river it is.

Over 900 per 100 ml is the line SwimSignal's E. coli estimate is about. It is the inland "sufficient" standard's line for a bathing water's long-run rating, not a limit on any one sample.

Sources

The licence. The company's published layer. SwimSignal kept only the E. coli results at these three points, joined positions a few metres apart into one point, and named each point after its spot. The counts, dates and limits are as published.
