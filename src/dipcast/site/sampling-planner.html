Sampling planner

What this rests on

  • The simulation's world was made up to match the model's own form. Real counts, real differences between sites and real rain forecasts do not follow it, so a real test would be harder.
  • No policy here has been tried in the field. Nobody has sampled to any of these rules and compared the result.
  • The cost is the lab's price per sample only, and you set it. Travel between scattered sites, and staff on call to sample at short notice after rain, are not counted.
  • "After rain" means more than 10 mm in the 48 hours before the sample, from archived forecast rain, not a rain gauge. Another line would give other counts.
  • The samples are the Environment Agency's routine ones, on days it fixed in advance. They show where the model's data are thin, not where the water is worse.

What would show a policy works

Only a field study. A sampler with a budget would take samples by one rule in some weeks and by their usual schedule in others, with each week's rule drawn at random before the season. The models updated with each set of samples would then be scored on later samples that neither rule chose. The test, its pass rule and its size would be written down before the first sample. Until then, no policy here can be called more efficient. The study in full.
