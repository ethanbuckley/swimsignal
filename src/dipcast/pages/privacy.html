Privacy notice

What SwimSignal collects, and what it does not. Last updated 29 October 2026.

Who is responsible

SwimSignal is run by one person, Ethan Buckley (ethanbuckley on GitHub), who is the controller for the personal data described here.

For anything private, including a data-protection request or a complaint, email hello@swimsignal.co.uk. A complaint is acknowledged within 30 days. For general questions you can also open an issue on the project's GitHub repository; issues are public, so do not put anything personal in one.

The short version

  • No accounts, no cookies and no advertising. There is no analytics script and no third-party tracking.
  • Your saved spots, your last plan, your swim journal and your location stay on your device.
  • Opening the site sends your IP address to the services that supply its files, as it would for any website. They are listed below.

Who sees your IP address

When you open the site, your browser fetches files from these services, and each one receives your IP address and basic details of your browser:

  • GitHub, which hosts the site (GitHub Pages). GitHub logs these requests for its own security purposes and is responsible for those logs. Its privacy statement applies.
  • The OpenStreetMap Foundation, for the map images. Its privacy policy applies.

One more service is asked only when you choose to, on the Coverage page or on a bathing water's page: the data service at environment.data.gov.uk. Opening the list of Welsh bathing waters on the Coverage page asks it for Natural Resources Wales's current samples and forecasts. Opening "Today's EA advice", under an English bathing water on the Coverage page or on a bathing water's page, loads the Environment Agency's own panel for that site from it; the page loads the panel with scripts switched off and does not tell it which page you were on. Each time, the service receives your IP address and basic details of your browser. SwimSignal sends it nothing else. Natural Resources Wales's privacy notice and the Environment Agency's personal information charter apply.

SwimSignal asks for these files because the page cannot show its map or run without them. That is the legitimate interest these requests rest on. SwimSignal itself receives no personal data from them and keeps no logs. These services may handle your IP address outside the UK, under their own safeguards.

Your location

The list of spots can be sorted by distance from you, and Plan a swim can start from where you are. Your browser asks for your location only if you choose "Near me" on the list or "Use my location" on Plan a swim. The page uses it to order the spots by distance, and on Plan a swim to leave out those too far away, and for nothing else: it is not sent to SwimSignal or to anyone else, it is not stored, and it is gone when you close the page. A plan's address says "here" in place of where you are, so a link to it does not hold your location. Neither choice moves the map, so the map images fetched from OpenStreetMap do not reveal it either.

A town, village or district you type on Plan a swim is looked up on your device, in a list of places the page fetches from this site; what you type is not sent anywhere. The place goes into the page's address after a #, which browsers do not send to the site, so a link you share holds it. The map then moves to it, so the map images fetched from OpenStreetMap are of that area. The plan you last looked at (its day, that place, how far, the kind of water, any spots you compared, and the time, spot, backup spot, finish, what it is for and where to meet of an outing you chose, but never your own location) is also kept in your browser's local storage, so Plan a swim opens on it when you come back. It stays on your device and is not sent anywhere; clearing this site's data removes it. So that the outing can say what has changed since you planned it, your browser also keeps a note of what its spot and backup spot showed then: the level for the outing's day, any official notice and whether its reviewer marked it as restricting swimming, the river level at the spot's gauge, which forecast that was and when you planned. The note is kept in your browser's local storage and sent nowhere. It is replaced when you change the outing's day, spot or backup, removed when you clear the outing's spot, and clearing this site's data removes it too.

"Share this outing", on Plan a swim, makes a link holding the outing's day, its time, its spot and backup spot, the spot where a paddle finishes, what it is for (swimming, paddling or paddleboarding) and where to meet (the spot's parking or where you get in), as you picked them, with the forecast's issue time and each spot's level for that day when you shared it. It starts the plan from the outing's spot, so it never holds where you start from, and it holds no words anyone typed. All of it is after a #, which browsers do not send to the site. Opening such a link shows the outing without keeping it: the plan kept in your browser, and the note about what its outing showed, stay as they were until you press "Keep this outing", which puts the shared outing in their place.

Saved spots

If you press Save on a spot, its identifier is kept in your browser's local storage, so the spot shows on your Saved page and at the top of the list. It stays on your device: it is not sent to SwimSignal or to anyone else. Clearing this site's data in your browser removes it. On an iPhone, Safari and a Home Screen app keep separate lists. If you make more than one list of saved spots, the names you give them are kept the same way, with which spots are in each.

So that a saved spot's card can say what has changed since you last looked, your browser also keeps a note for each saved spot of what it last showed you: the spot's level, how many overflows upstream were discharging, its Environment Agency rating, any official notice it showed and whether its reviewer marked it as restricting swimming, which forecast that was and when you looked. The note is kept in your browser's local storage and sent nowhere. It is removed when you remove the spot, and clearing this site's data removes it too.

"Share this list" makes a link holding the list's name and the identifiers of its spots, after a # that browsers do not send to the site. "Share this plan", on Plan a swim, makes the same kind of link for the spots the plan shows as nothing flagged, with the plan's day; it never holds where the plan starts from. Anyone you send a link to can see the list's name and which spots are on it. Opening such a link shows the list without keeping it; it is kept in your browser only if you press "Add to my lists".

On a decision record, "I have read version …" keeps a note in your browser's local storage of which spot, which day and which version of the record you ticked, and when. It is sent nowhere, so nobody else can see it; clearing this site's data removes it.

"Log a swim", on a spot's page, keeps a swim journal the same way: the spot, the day and time, what the forecast showed then, and any note, minutes in the water and photos you add, in your browser's storage on this device (the photos in its IndexedDB), sent nowhere. You can delete a swim, or all of them, on the Saved page; clearing this site's data removes them too. "Save a copy" makes a file of the journal on your device, and "Add swims from a copy" reads one there; SwimSignal receives neither. "Share as a picture" draws the forecast into an image on your device; nothing is uploaded until you choose where to send it.

The swim passport on the Saved page is worked out from that journal each time the page opens: your outings, places and badges. It keeps only the day you first planned an outing with a backup, in your browser's storage on this device, sent nowhere. Sharing a badge sends nothing by itself: it opens your device's share sheet with the badge's name and this site's address, never a place or a date.

Offline copy

After your first visit, the browser keeps a copy of the page, its icons, the map library and the latest forecast (using a service worker), so the site still opens without signal. The copy holds only what every visitor downloads, and nothing about you. You can turn it off, which also deletes it, under "Saved spots, offline use and your data" at the bottom of the home page. Clearing this site's data removes it too. If you turn it off, that choice is kept in your browser's local storage, so the copy is not made again; it is sent nowhere.

"Keep this outing for offline use", on an outing on Plan a swim, stores more of the same kind of copy on your device: the plan's page, the pages and files of the outing's spot and backup spot, the forecast and the site's scripts. With them, your browser's local storage notes the outing's day, its spot and backup spot, when you kept it and when its forecast was issued. These are files every visitor downloads, and keeping them sends nothing to SwimSignal or anyone else. They are deleted after the outing's day, when you press "Stop keeping it", when you turn off the offline copy, or when you delete this site's data. On iPhone and iPad, Safari can delete them sooner if the device is short of space, and the Home Screen app keeps its own copy, apart from Safari's.

If you email

If you email the operator, your address and what you write are used only to reply and to deal with any request or complaint. The basis is legitimate interests, or a legal obligation for a data-protection request. They are kept only as long as that takes, then deleted, and are never shared or sold. Email to hello@swimsignal.co.uk is held by Apple, in the operator's iCloud Mail account, in the meantime.

Feedback

The feedback form prepares an email on your device. It does not submit or save what you type. Nothing is sent to the operator until you send the email yourself; the email handling described above then applies. The copy button puts your draft on your device’s clipboard. You can instead open a public issue on GitHub; GitHub’s privacy statement applies to it. Do not put personal information in a public issue.

Practical guides

If you email details or photos for a spot's practical guide, the email is handled as above, with one addition: what you send may be published on the spot's page. A detail is shown as "A swimmer's suggestion", with the day and the name or initials you asked to be credited as, or "a swimmer" if you did not say. A photo is published only if your email says you took it, that it may be published with your credit, and that anyone who can be recognised in it is happy to be online. Before it is published, the photo is shrunk and everything else in the file is removed, including where it was taken and the phone's details. The basis is your consent. You can withdraw it at any time by email, and the operator then takes the detail or photo off the site. Guides are part of SwimSignal's public source code on GitHub, so an earlier version stays in its history.

Research sessions

From time to time the operator runs test sessions, in which a swimmer or a group leader uses the site while thinking aloud, to see whether its pages are understood. Taking part is optional and arranged directly with the operator. If you take part, the operator keeps:

  • your contact details, to arrange the session and, if you agree, to send two short follow-up emails, one and two weeks later, asking whether you used the site;
  • notes of what you did and said in the session, under a code (such as P03), not your name;
  • a record of what you agreed to;
  • a recording of the screen and your voice, only if you agree to it separately.

Nothing is asked about your health, your home address or where you are. The notes are kept under a code, not your name, but what you said or did and the date of your session can still identify you, especially in a small group. So they are treated as personal data for as long as they are kept, including after your contact details are deleted. They are used only to find out what on the site is misunderstood and to change it.

Only genuinely anonymised findings are published: identifying details are removed, and the operator checks that you could not be recognised from what is published, taking into account how few people take part. Your words are quoted only if you agreed, and only if the quote cannot identify you.

The basis for the session, the notes, any recording and the follow-up emails is your consent. The record of what you agreed to is kept on the basis of legitimate interests, to show what you agreed to.

All of it is kept in a private folder on the operator's own computer. It is not published, and it is not part of SwimSignal's public source code. It is not shared or sold. If the session is a video call, the call service handles the call under its own privacy policy.

How long each is kept:

  • Contact details: until your last follow-up email is answered, or 21 days after your session, whichever comes first.
  • A recording: until the notes have been checked against it, and no more than 7 days.
  • Notes and the record of what you agreed to: until the summary of all the sessions is written, and no later than 31 January 2027, unless you agreed to them being kept longer.

You can withdraw at any time, during or after the session, by emailing hello@swimsignal.co.uk with your code or the date of your session. The operator then deletes your notes, any recording and your contact details. Findings already published in genuinely anonymised form stay, since they cannot identify you. If anything published could identify you, such as a quote or a detail, it is removed or corrected under your rights to erasure and to object.

If alerts are added

A planned feature would send a notification to your phone or computer when a saved spot's forecast reaches high or very high risk. If it is added, this notice will be updated first. It would store only what is needed to send those notifications, never share or sell it, and you would be able to turn it off at any time, which deletes it.

The server version

SwimSignal's code can also run as a server that forecasts any point you click. No such server runs for this site. Whoever runs one receives the coordinates you click and your IP address, and keeps a log of the forecasts issued (the coordinates, the time and the forecast values, with no IP address or identifier) to score their accuracy.

Your rights

UK data protection law gives you rights over personal data about you, including to be told what is held, to have it corrected or deleted, and to object. Apart from what is kept for research sessions (above), SwimSignal holds none, as described above; ask GitHub about its logs. Details and photos swimmers send for a practical guide are published as described under Practical guides. You can complain to the operator by email (above) and to the Information Commissioner's Office.

The operator is an individual, not a company. This notice describes current practice in plain English. It was checked against UK data protection law and the ICO's guidance on 29 September 2026, but not by a lawyer.
