Data files

The files the site is built from are public: every spot's forecast, the storm overflows on the map and the accuracy scores. This page lists each one, what it holds and the licence it is under.

In short

  • The files are in the site's data/ folder, in JSON, GeoJSON and CSV, with packed river data for forecasts at a clicked point. The table below links them. Each build writes the current forecasts again.
  • Keep the files' credits, or the credits on the terms page, with anything you publish from them. The CSV carries its credits in comment lines; the packed river data uses the credits in anypoint/tiles.json.
  • The data from the water companies, the Environment Agency and the other providers keeps its own licence. SwimSignal's own forecasts, levels and scores may be reused for non-commercial purposes with credit; ask first about any other use.
  • The same levels are in Atom feeds, for every spot, each area and each spot on its own.
  • The levels are forecasts, not water tests.

coastal.json

An official-information snapshot, separate from SwimSignal’s inland forecasts. fetched_at, status, source, licence and credit describe the fetch. catalogue_state distinguishes live names/ratings from a dated fallback, whose source age is in catalogue_fetched_at. The fallback never contains current advice. Each entry in sites has an EA id, name, kind (coast or estuary), official profile, historical rating with its year and source, and advice.

Each site also has sample: its latest statutory sample in the EA Water Quality Archive, with taken_at (UK time with offset), ecoli and, from the same sample, enterococci (intestinal enterococci; null when the archive has none), each as value per 100 ml and qualifier (=, or < for below the detection limit), and the archive's point. Its state is ok, none (no sample since samples.since), unmapped (no archive point matched with confidence) or unavailable (the archive did not answer). The top-level samples gives the source, when it was asked (fetched_at), the window start and whether the answer is cached. The archive can lag the official profile by several days, and one sample is not a rating or today's water.

Each site also has a provisional water temperature, never part of any rating or advice. water_temp_est has temp_c, its date, route (sea_air_curve, or estuary_air_curve for an estuary), tested (always false), typical_c, air_7d_c and air_km. It is the sea curve applied to the last 7 days' air temperature at the nearest weather-model square the click-anywhere forecast already reads, with that square's distance from the site's sampling point. typical_c is the typical error at sea points in a May to September test (1.63 °C, exploratory), and null in other months and at estuaries, where none was measured. A site without one has water_temp_est_missing with why: air (no square nearby in this build), point (no sampling point on file) or error. The sampling points come from the EA's and NRW's bathing-water service (data/raw/bathing_water_points.json in the repository, same licence).

Advice states distinguish increased risk, no increased risk forecast, no forecast, no current advice and unavailability. Valid advice retains its origin, prediction time, publication time, expiry time and source. An increased-risk record in force counts whatever its origin, and stands over a normal one in force at the same time: the EA's notice after a pollution incident has no origin (null). Check expiry again when using the file: a snapshot can outlive its advice. Missing advice, a normal incident record and a historical rating do not establish clean water.

Licence. Environment Agency copyright and/or database right, Open Government Licence v3.0. Keep the credit and source links. The EA does not endorse SwimSignal.

wales.json

Natural Resources Wales's designated bathing waters, separate from SwimSignal's forecasts: SwimSignal makes no Welsh forecast. state is live when NRW's service answered the build, cached when the build used the dated snapshot kept in the repository (NRW's service refused GitHub's runners on 3 October 2026), or unavailable; fetched_at is when those sites and samples were read. Each entry in sites has NRW's id, name, kind (coast, estuary, lake or river), official profile, latest rating with its year (null for a water not yet rated) and sample: the latest in-season sample, with taken_at, ecoli and enterococci as value per 100 ml and qualifier. The file holds no pollution-risk forecasts: the coverage page asks NRW for those in the reader's browser, and none is kept. One sample is not a rating or today's water. Each site also has a provisional water temperature, water_temp_est or water_temp_est_missing, as in coastal.json, with lake_air_curve and river_air_curve for its inland waters (their curves' summer test figures, 1.93 and 1.85 °C, in May to September).

Licence. Contains Natural Resources Wales information © Natural Resources Wales and Database Right. All rights reserved. Open Government Licence v3.0. NRW does not endorse SwimSignal.

scotland.json

SEPA's designated bathing waters, separate from SwimSignal's forecasts: SwimSignal makes no Scottish forecast. state is live or unavailable (with error); fetched_at is when SEPA's layer was read. Each entry in sites has an id made from SEPA's location code, name, SEPA's page for it, its point (lat, lon) and its latest rating with the year, or null with unrated_year when SEPA lists it as unclassified. It holds no samples, signs or advice: those are on SEPA's page for each water, under SEPA's own conditions. Each also has kind (coast or lake), with kind_from name: SEPA's layer does not type its waters, so Loch Morlich is a lake by its name and the rest are coast. And a provisional water temperature, water_temp_est or water_temp_est_missing, as in coastal.json.

Licence. Contains public sector information licensed under the Open Government Licence v2.0: SEPA bathing water points. SEPA does not endorse SwimSignal.

ireland.json

The bathing waters the Environmental Protection Agency (EPA) lists for the Republic of Ireland, separate from SwimSignal's forecasts: SwimSignal makes no forecast in Ireland. state is live when every part was current for this build, partial when a part was kept from an earlier build or could not be read, or unavailable (with error). locations, samples and alerts each give their own state (ok, kept or unavailable) and when they were read (fetched_at). The list of waters is read once a day and the samples at most every three hours; restrictions are read on every build and never kept. Each water also has its point, lat and lon (from the EPA's Irish Grid easting and northing), kind (coast, estuary, lake or river) with kind_from: epa_code where the designated water's code says it, else name (a Lough, Lake or River is a lake). And a provisional water temperature, water_temp_est or water_temp_est_missing, as in coastal.json.

Each entry in sites has the EPA's id, name (as the EPA gives it, Irish accents kept), county, designated (false for a water the council monitors that is not a designated bathing water), its EPA page on beaches.ie, its latest rating with the year (null when none is listed, with unrated_year when the EPA lists it as not classified), season_restriction (a restriction for the whole season), alerts (restrictions in force: type in the EPA's words, since, cause and notice; null when the list could not be read) and sample: the latest sample this year, with date, season (in, 22 May to 15 September, or out, voluntary and not used for a rating), ecoli and enterococci as value per 100 ml and qualifier, and status, the EPA's word for that sample. A sample's state is ok, none (none listed for samples.season) or unavailable. One sample is not a rating or today's water.

Licence. Contains bathing water data from the Environmental Protection Agency (Ireland), Bathing Water Open Data API, licensed under CC BY 4.0. SwimSignal shows some of the fields and picks each water's latest sample. The EPA does not endorse SwimSignal.

northern_ireland.json

The bathing waters of the Department of Agriculture, Environment and Rural Affairs (DAERA) in Northern Ireland, separate from SwimSignal's forecasts: SwimSignal makes no forecast in Northern Ireland. state is live, kept (DAERA did not answer, so the copy from an earlier build, at most three days old, with fetched_at its time) or unavailable (with error). Each entry in sites has an id made from DAERA's site code, name, kind (coast or inland), candidate, inactive, DAERA's profile of the water, the indicator (DAERA's code and its words for it) and sampled_at, the sampling time DAERA gives with the indicator (UK time with its offset). It holds no rain figures and no samples. Each also has its point, lat and lon, and a provisional water temperature, water_temp_est or water_temp_est_missing, as in coastal.json; the one inland water is estimated as a lake.

Licence. Contains public sector information licensed under the Open Government Licence v3.0: DAERA bathing water monitoring points. DAERA does not endorse SwimSignal.

spots.json

Every spot's forecast, as the map and the spot pages show it. A spot's level (low, moderate, high or very high) is worked out from these fields by the page's own rules, levels.js: the worst of the sewage spill risk, the E. coli estimate from May to September, a "poor" rating and an algae check from the last 14 days. A day's label is the spill forecast's level alone.

generated_at
When the forecast was issued, UK time with its offset (ISO 8601)
build
The build's own checks: how many spots got a forecast, how many rows each water company's live feed returned, and any warnings
lead_skill
How good a forecast for each of the five days has been, as a share of the same-day forecast's skill: spill and water, five numbers each
credits
The data credits (below)
n, version
How many spots the file holds, and the version number of SwimSignal's code
spots
One entry for each spot, with the fields that follow
id, name, kind, river, source, lat, lon
From the spot list: its id (its page is spot/<id>/), its name, river or lake, the river's name, its source (designated for an Environment Agency bathing water, openstreetmap for a mapped swim spot), and where it is, in degrees (WGS84). Inclusion does not confirm permission or safe access
notes, query
The spot list's note on the spot, and the point and time the forecast was made for (lat, lon, issued_at)
assumptions, ecoli_scope
The model's settings for this forecast (flow speeds, die-off, how far upstream it looks, the models' versions); when the oldest rain forecast it used was fetched (rain_issued_at) and how many hours before the issue that was (rain_age_h), with rain_fallback where that rain came from the cache because Open-Meteo did not send a newer one (no answer) or the day's limit of requests held the request back (daily limit), and rain_mixed where the rest of the rain is newer; ecoli_model, the E. coli model that gave the figures (file, n_samples, loyo_brier; for a river spot on the soil-moisture model, also its test; soil_source, the live soil-moisture request the value came from; fitted_on_soil_source, the archive the model was fitted on; and today's model under baseline); ecoli_model.soil, whether a river spot's E. coli estimate used soil moisture (used; sm, the value in m³/m³; at, the hour it is for, 00:00 UTC on the issue day), or why not (reason), when it falls back to the model without it; and where the E. coli estimate has been tested: which kind of water and which months
days
One entry for each day: date; risk, the sewage spill risk as a fraction (0.4 is 40 on the site's 0–100 scale), and label, its level, from the score the site shows (that fraction times 100, rounded: 15 and over is moderate risk, 40 high risk, 70 very high risk); expected_spilling_overflows, how many of the overflows upstream that count (see upstream_summary) are expected to spill; p_ecoli_gt900, the E. coli estimate, the chance a sample would show over 900 E. coli per 100 ml; in_validated_season, false from October to April, when that estimate is untested; rain_48h_mm, the rain at the spot in the 48 hours to midday, and rain_48h_coverage, the share of those hours with rain data; data_status, whether the rain forecast was complete, and rain_missing_share, the share of the day's spill forecast that rests on days without rain data; band, where the build had them, the lowest (low) and highest (high) sewage spill risk from three Met Office ensemble rain forecasts, under test and shown on days 3 to 5 only (Method)
now
Right now, from the live feeds: risk and label, as for a day; how many overflows upstream are discharging (discharging_upstream), stopped lately and still counted (recent_upstream: a spill counts until assumptions.recent_spill_hours after its water has passed the spot) and report live (monitored_upstream: discharging, stopped lately, or not discharging on a current feed), and any company feed that is down (feed_down) or has not updated within 6 hours (feed_stale, with its last update, and stale_upstream, how many of its overflows here), and how many do not report because their company has no live feed (no_feed_upstream). While right now's risk is moderate or worse, or an overflow upstream is discharging, where its risk comes from (source) and, when spills whose water has not reached the spot yet hold at least half of it, the largest of them (arriving, with arrives_at, when its water should get there). Where right now's risk is moderate or worse, when it should be back to low risk if no new spill starts (clears_at, and what sets that time, clears_by)
upstream_summary
How many monitored overflows can reach the spot and count (overflows) and how many of them report live. An overflow counts where its model weight here is 0.5% or more (a weight of 0.005: less shows as 0%); the counts in days and now count only these. too_far: how many more are upstream but carry too little weight here to count (too far, or too small beside the river here), and where there is one, which (too_far_overflow). Where none reaches 0.5%, all count
contributors
Up to 10 of those overflows, the ones that matter most to the spot: the company's id and name for it, its distance and travel time, weight, the chance that a spill there affects the spot after travel time, die-off and dilution, and its chance of spilling on each day (p_spill_days); its status and data_state as in overflows.geojson, and feed_updated_at, when its company's feed last updated (UTC); for an overflow that spilled on many of its dry days, dry_weather: the year, its dry spill-days (days) and its dry days (dry_days), as the method defines them. Not part of the level
location
Where the spot sits on the river network: the watercourse, whether it is treated as a river or a lake, and how far it was moved to reach the network
catchment, linked
How the spot's water is joined to the other spots', for the reasons on a spot page's Nearby card. catchment: a short id for where the river from the spot reaches tidal water, or the end of OS Open Rivers' network; spots with different ids drain apart. linked: the other spots joined to it by river, each with s (at least one of the same monitored overflows is counted at both), d (downstream of it) or u (upstream of it), the last two with no overflow counted at both. In the app's files linked is in spot/<id>.json, not in spots-lite.json. Both absent for a spot with no place on the network, such as an isolated lake
classification, algae
At bathing waters: the Environment Agency's rating, with earlier years and a link to its page, and its sampler's latest algae check
lab_sample
At bathing waters: the Environment Agency's latest E. coli lab sample that SwimSignal holds, when it was taken (UK time with its offset, taken_at), the count per 100 ml (ecoli) and, where the count is a limit, qualifier (< or >). Null where there is no sample this season. The EA publishes samples days after they are taken
measured
Lab results an organisation sent SwimSignal for this spot, taken in the last 30 days, newest first: when the sample was taken (UK time with its offset, sampled_at), the count per 100 ml of E. coli (ecoli) and of intestinal enterococci (enterococci), each with ecoli_qualifier or enterococci_qualifier (< or >) where the count is a limit; who sent it (supplier) and when (received); and, where the sender gives them, the lab (lab), whether the sender says it is UKAS-accredited (ukas), and the method (method). Only results the sender agreed to publish. SwimSignal did not take or check them. Absent where there are none. Not part of the level. In spots.json and spot/<id>.json, not in spots-lite.json
water_body
The Environment Agency water body the spot is in: its id and name in the Catchment Data Explorer, whose page for it is https://environment.data.gov.uk/catchment-planning/WaterBody/<id>, and, where the Environment Agency lists agriculture and rural land management as a reason it does not reach good status under "Pollution from rural areas", how certain it is of that (agriculture: confirmed or probable). Absent where the spot is in no water body the Explorer lists reasons for. Not part of the level
river_state
The nearest Environment Agency level gauge: its latest reading, its usual range and how old the reading is, and where the gauge is (station_lat, station_lon, from the Environment Agency's station record). Not part of the level
flow_state, flood_alerts, flood_alerts_other, flood_alerts_unmatched
Separate from pollution risk: high or rising fast when a recent gauge on the spot's river meets those thresholds, otherwise null; and the Environment Agency flood alerts and warnings in force whose flood area's outline covers the spot's point on its water, or reaches its lake. An empty alert list means none applied; null means the check failed. flood_alerts_other counts alerts in force for other water (another river, the coast) and flood_alerts_unmatched those that could not be matched to the spot (its own river or lake but not its stretch, or no outline). flood_alerts_search says which search an empty list comes from: england, the national list with nothing in force anywhere in England, or within_10km, the Environment Agency's own search of the spot within 10 km; null when unchecked. These do not change the pollution level
water_temp
Where there is a recent Environment Agency sensor on the same river: the temperature in Celsius (temp_c), its time, age and quality, the station and its link, and its distance and direction from the spot. This is a reading at that sensor, not an estimate of the temperature at the swim spot. Absent where no suitable sensor was found
water_temp_est
River and lake spots, all year: an estimate of the water near the surface today, in whole degrees Celsius (temp_c), the day it is for (date), how it was made (route), whether that route was tested for this water and season (tested), the typical error the test measured for it (typical_c, null where none was measured) and the 7-day air it rests on (air_7d_c). Rivers from May to September are the tested model (route river_model, with within_c 2: the test); lakes, and every water from October to April, are provisional (the routes and what is known about their error). An estimate, not a measurement, and not part of the pollution level.
water_temp_est_missing
Where a river or lake spot has no estimate in this update: why (why: air, not all 30 days of air arrived; error) and the day (date). An older estimate is never given in its place.
weather
Each day's high, sunrise and sunset, from Open-Meteo
error
Instead of a forecast, where there is none: the reason

Licence. SwimSignal's forecasts, made from all the sources the credits list. The ratings, algae checks, water bodies, river levels, flood alerts and water temperatures are the Environment Agency's (OGL v3.0); the rainfall figures stay under CC BY-SA 4.0. OpenStreetMap spot records are kept in a separate source file under ODbL 1.0.

spots-lite.json and spot/

The same forecast as spots.json, split for the app. spots-lite.json is spots.json without each spot's contributors and guide, which only a spot's own page shows; they were half its size. Each spot in it has detail_file, its file in spot/, relative to data/, and, where it has monitored overflows upstream, reach_by_company: whose they are, as [company, share] pairs, the share of their summed weight, largest first. That file, spot/<id>.json, has the spot's id, generated_at, credits, contributors and guide, as in spots.json. spots.json stays whole: read it if you want everything in one file.

Beside it, spot/<id>-trace.json is the river network the forecast traced upstream of the spot: every river and stream within max_upstream_km (60 km) along the water, the water that reaches the spot, which its map draws. lines are the network's lines as encoded polylines (latitude and longitude to five decimal places), simplified to within simplified_m metres. overflows lists the monitored overflows counted on them, each as [site_id, latitude, longitude]. cut_ends lists where the trace stops a river that goes on, at max_upstream_km, each as [latitude, longitude]; an empty list means every river traced reaches its source within it. A spot with nothing traced (an isolated lake) has no trace file.

Licence. As spots.json. The traced rivers are from OS Open Rivers: contains OS data © Crown copyright and database right 2026.

alerts.json

Each spot's level and headline, worked out by the same rules as the page, for the alert service. The smallest file, for anyone who wants only the answer.

generated_at, credits
As in spots.json
spots
Keyed by spot id: name; level, one of low, moderate, high and very high, or "no overflows", "not covered" or "no forecast"; rank, 0 for low to 3 for very high and −1 without a level; headline, as the list shows it ("High risk today: sewage spills"); action, what to do at that level, the line under the answer on the spot's page; url, the spot's page; best, only where the level changes from day to day: the lowest level of the four days after today, the first date with it, and the words the weekly note uses ("Saturday, low risk")

Licence. SwimSignal's levels and headlines, made from the same sources as spots.json.

Feeds

The same levels and headlines as Atom feeds, for a feed reader, Slack's RSS app, a Discord bot or IFTTT. Each time the forecast is issued, a feed gets a new entry for each of its spots: the spot's name and headline as the title, the headline and what to do as the summary, the spot's page as the link, its level as the category, and the time the forecast was issued as updated. The feed's rights carries the credits.

  • feed.xml: every spot.
  • spot/<id>/feed.xml: one spot, beside its page (for the spot at spot/<id>/).
  • One for each area in the list:

A reader that checks every 30 minutes misses nothing: the forecast is issued at most that often. The feeds keep only the latest forecast, so a reader that checks less often skips the ones in between.

Licence. As alerts.json.

overflows.geojson

Every storm overflow on the map, as a GeoJSON point (longitude, latitude), with what its water company's live feed last said about it. The largest file.

The same overflows are in overflows/ cut into map tiles, the ones the map loads: index.json gives the zoom and lists the tiles that have any, and each tile, <x>-<y>.json, is a GeoJSON FeatureCollection of the overflows in that Web Mercator tile.

site_id, company, site_name
The water company's id and name for the overflow
receiving_watercourse
The river or stream it discharges into, as the company names it; null where the feed names none or gives a placeholder such as "#N/A"
status
1 discharging; 0 not discharging; −1 monitor offline; −2 no live feed: the overflow is in the Environment Agency's annual returns but in no live feed SwimSignal reads, as all of Dŵr Cymru Welsh Water's are; −3 unknown, because the company's feed returned nothing at the last poll (the spill times are from the poll before)
has_live
Whether the overflow is in its company's live feed
data_state
Whether its status is current: live, a status from a feed that updated in the 6 hours before the poll; stale, a status from a feed that did not, or gives no time, so "not discharging" may be out of date; offline, no status (−1, −3, or −2 at a company that has a live feed); no_feed, its company has no live feed. On a stale one, feed_updated_at is when its feed last updated (UTC), null if the feed gives no time
latest_event_start, latest_event_end
Its latest spill, as the company's feed gives it (UTC)
lta_spills
Its long-term average number of spills a year, from the Environment Agency's annual returns

Licence. The water companies' data, CC BY 4.0, via the National Storm Overflow Hub, with the Environment Agency's annual returns (OGL v3.0), combined by SwimSignal.

verification.json

Everything on the Accuracy page, which explains each table: live, the scores of the forecasts this site has issued, against what the overflows then did and, for the E. coli estimate, against the Environment Agency's samples (live.ecoli_live); the level each spot's page showed, against the level the spills that happened would have given (live.spots_live); holdout, leads and reliability, the test on a held-out year, 2025; lead_calibration; ecoli, ecoli_combined and ecoli_model, the E. coli estimate's tests; sampling_plan; and credits.

Licence. SwimSignal's scores, made against the water companies' discharge records (CC BY 4.0) and the Environment Agency's samples (OGL v3.0).

verification_live.csv

One row for each scored overflow, day and forecast lead, so the live spill scores can be checked independently. It is published only when its row count matches live.n_scored in verification.json. Read it as CSV after skipping lines beginning with #, which hold its credits.

overflow_id, company, day
The overflow, its water company and the day being forecast
lead, issued_at
Days ahead (0 is the same day), and when the scored forecast was issued
forecast_raw, forecast_calibrated, climatology
Spill probabilities, from 0 to 1: before and after lead calibration, and the historical baseline used for comparison
observed
1 if a spill was observed that day, 0 if none was observed; days without sufficient observations are excluded

Licence. SwimSignal's forecast and scoring rows, with the water companies' discharge records (CC BY 4.0).

verification_spots_live.csv

One row for each spot with a monitored overflow upstream, each day and each forecast lead 0 to 4, from the first day the forecasts logged each overflow's travel time, so the level a spot's page shows can be checked against what happened. The rules were fixed in advance in the pre-registration (amendment 1). It is published only when its row count matches live.spots_live.csv.rows in verification.json. Values have 6 decimal places, and the Accuracy page's figures are computed from them as written. Skip the lines beginning with #, which hold its credits.

spot_id, day, lead
The spot, the day being forecast and the days ahead (0 is the same day)
issued_at, forecast, version
When the forecast was issued (the latest by 08:00 on the issue day), the spot's sewage spill exposure index from 0 to 1, and the model's stamp. Empty when no forecast was out by 08:00
observed
The same index with the spills that happened in place of the forecast ones, each overflow's spill days moved later by its travel time in that forecast. Empty when the spot's overflows were not observed well enough to say
climatology
The same index from each overflow's long-run spill rate for the month instead of its forecast: the reference the forecast is compared with
covered, reason
The share of the spot's weight whose overflows were observed on every day that reaches it (0.95 is needed), and why there is no observation

Licence. SwimSignal's forecast and scoring rows, built from the water companies' discharge records (CC BY 4.0).

spot_scores.json

The rows of verification_live.csv for each spot's upstream overflows, counted, for the "How the forecast has done here" tile on its page. Nothing is weighted, so each count can be found by filtering the CSV to the overflows in the spot's upstream/ file. A spot with no monitored overflow upstream is left out. The file has warn_at, the spill chance counted as a warning (the Accuracy page's); min_spills, the spill days a spot needs before its page gives a share; first_day and last_day; all, the same counts over every row; generated_at, credits and spots, by spot id:

overflows, scored_overflows
How many monitored overflows are upstream, and at how many any day has been scored
overflow_days, spill_days, days
The scored overflow-days (one overflow on one day), those with a spill, and the calendar days they span (first_day to last_day)
forecasts, hits, misses, false_alarms
The scored forecasts, up to five for each overflow-day (one a lead), and of them: warnings before a spill, spills without a warning, and warnings with no spill

Licence. SwimSignal's scores, made against the water companies' discharge records (CC BY 4.0).

upstream/

One file a spot, upstream/<id>.json, with every monitored storm overflow within 60 km upstream along the river network, largest weight first. spots.json keeps only the ten that matter most in the current forecast; the organisers' page loads a spot's file when it has more. A spot with nothing upstream has no file. Each file has the spot's id, generated_at, credits and overflows, one entry an overflow:

site_id, site_name, company, receiving_watercourse
The water company's id and name for the overflow, the company, and the water it discharges into, as the company names it (null where the feed names none)
distance_km, lake_distance_km, travel_h
How far upstream along rivers, how far across lakes on top of that (the two add up to the distance along the network), and how long sewage takes to arrive at the model's speeds
weight
A modelled relative contribution weight, 0 to 1: how much a spill there counts at the spot in the model, from its assumptions on travel time and die-off and on dilution. Neither a measured bacterial concentration nor a calibrated chance of contamination (how it is worked out)
lta_spills, spill_hours
Its long-term average spills a year, and the hours it spilled in its latest annual return (Environment Agency)
has_live, lat, lon
Whether its water company reports its status live, and where it is
status, data_state, feed_updated_at, latest_event_end
What its company's feed said at the last poll before the update: 1 discharging, 0 not discharging, -1 monitor offline, -2 no live feed, -3 the company's feed did not answer; whether that status was current (live), from a feed not updated within six hours (stale), missing (offline) or from a company with no live feed SwimSignal reads (no_feed); the feed's own update time; and when its last reported spill ended. Null in files from before 30 October 2026

anypoint/

The files the map uses to calculate a forecast at a point you click, on your device. A clicked-point forecast has no local river gauge or bathing-water sample attached to it: it is a sewage-spill forecast, and cannot confirm that swimming is safe.

places.json

The places Plan a swim can start from: every city, town, suburban area, village and other settlement in England and Wales in Ordnance Survey's OS Open Names, about 26,000 names. places lists each as [name, latitude, longitude], to two decimal places (about a kilometre), cities first and villages last. A name that several places share keeps it bare for the biggest, and the others carry their district or county after a comma. The page fetches the file the first time a place is typed, and looks the name up on the device. The file changes only when make_places.py is run again.

waters.json

The rivers and lakes the search on the home page finds on the map: Ordnance Survey's named rivers in OS Open Rivers with at least 1 km in England, and the Environment Agency's WFD lake water bodies in England, about 9,700 in all. waters lists each as [name, alternative, kind, near, south, west, north, east, latitude, longitude, km]: its other name if it has one (the Welsh name of the Wye, "Afon Gwy"), river or lake, the city or town nearest its middle, the box around it in England to two decimal places, a point on it, and its length in England in km. The pieces OS gives a river of one name are joined where they lie within 2 km of each other, so rivers that share a name stay apart ("River Derwent" near Wirksworth, Malton, Consett and three more). The page fetches the file the first time three letters are typed in the search and looks the name up on the device. The file changes only when make_waters.py is run again.

The credits field

The JSON files carry credits: attribution, the notices each provider asks for; modified, which says that SwimSignal combined and modelled the data and that no provider endorses it; licences, each licence's name and address; and full, the address of the full credits on the terms page. Individual river-link tiles carry the full credits' address; their binary index uses tiles.json's credits. The CSV's comment header gives the same notices. The MIT licence of the source code covers the code, not the data.

Using the files

  • A web page on another site can read the files: GitHub Pages sends the header that allows it (Access-Control-Allow-Origin: *, checked on 3 October 2026).
  • Browsers may keep a copy for 10 minutes, and the scheduled rebuild runs at most every 30 minutes, so asking more often gains nothing. generated_at in spots.json says when the forecast was issued.
  • The fields can change when the site does, without notice. This page describes the current ones; the build script writes them.
  • To show one spot's forecast on your own non-commercial site, there is a ready-made card: see About.
