A river's bad days

At rivers no model here had seen, did SwimSignal's estimate put the bad days in order, and did it know more than the rain?

Loading the two tests…

Tested, at monitoring points, not swim spots. Two frozen-model backtests: each statement was written down before any result was joined, and each was read once. On the day itself, with rain as it fell. It changes nothing in the forecast.

1

How to read it

read this first

Putting days in order. At each point, the Environment Agency took E. coli samples on many days. A figure "puts the days in order" if its higher values fall on the days with higher counts. The tests measured this with a correlation inside each point, averaged over the points: 1 is perfect order, 0 is no better than chance. The bar, set before either test, was 0.15.

Three figures were compared. SwimSignal's E. coli estimate, from the model file the site used before soil moisture was added in season. Rain in the 48 hours before the sample, alone. And the overflow figure alone: the spill chances of the overflows upstream, weighted by the model's distance and dilution. In a test on past days, those spill chances come from the same rain, so the three are close to one signal.

What "over 900" means. 900 E. coli per 100 ml is the line for an inland bathing water to be rated "sufficient". It is a line, not a safety boundary. These points are not bathing waters, and about half their results were over it.

Not swim spots. The points are the Environment Agency's river monitoring points, chosen for its own work, many of them in planned investigations. No model here had seen their results before each test.

What it changed on the site

Only the order passed, so only the order is shown. A river spot's page gives one sentence: whether today's estimate is higher or lower than usual for that place. It changes no level. In season it sits beside the percentage (decided 17 November 2026, shown from May 2027). From October to April, when the percentage stays hidden, it is shown alone, switched on 25 November 2026 after the winter test. The summer proposal, the winter line.

The level a spot shows still comes from the spill forecast. Neither test supports a count or a range for a river, and neither shows that the estimate knows more than the rain.

What it cannot tell you

  • Nothing about a swim spot directly. The points are monitoring points. Most are in the South West, and none is a bathing water.
  • Nothing about days ahead. Both tests used rain as it fell, on the day of the sample. The site's forecast for later days rests on forecast rain, which was not tested here.
  • Nothing about live spill reports. On the day, the forecast can use an overflow's live status. A test on past days cannot, so whether that adds to rain is not known.
  • Not "safe". A day in the lower part of a place's order can still be over 900. About half of all results here were.
  • Not the in-season model with soil moisture. Both tests scored the file without it. In season the site uses the version with soil moisture, for rivers (PR #361); whether it keeps the order is not tested here.
  • Nothing about lakes or the sea. Both tests were at rivers only.
  • Official advice comes first. Environment Agency notices and signs at the water are on each spot page of the live site.

Questions worth asking next

proposed, not started

  1. Does the order hold at swim spots that are not bathing waters?

    Useful if: samples at such spots, taken on days fixed in advance, with a local group. Unconvincing if: another reading of these monitoring points.

  2. Is rain alone enough at a river with no overflow upstream?

    Useful if: a rain-only line, written down first and scored once on the odd winters, which are sealed for one test. Unconvincing if: a rule tuned on the even winters shown here.

  3. Does a forecast made days ahead keep the order?

    Useful if: forecast rain as it was issued, archived by run, scored on new samples. Unconvincing if: rain as it fell, relabelled as a forecast.
