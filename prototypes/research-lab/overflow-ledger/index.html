1

How to read it

read this first

A weight is the model's factor, from 0 to 1. For one overflow at one spot the forecast multiplies a survival factor (how long the model thinks the water takes to arrive) by a dilution factor. That product is the weight. It is not the share of a spill that arrives, and nobody measured it.

The ledger adds the weights up. For each spot: how many overflows the model traces to it, their weights added (the summed weight), the largest single weight, and how much of the sum that largest one carries. The forecast uses the same weights, overflow by overflow, when it turns spill chances into a spot's level.

How far to trust the chain.

What no row says. No row says an overflow caused a sample result, put any share of the bacteria at a spot, or broke a permit. The model holds no spill volume, and no treated sewage, farm or road runoff.

A season count, not shown here

development, seen data

A second calculation counted, for the 2024 and 2025 bathing seasons, the spot-days the model would have put at high level with each overflow and below high without it, using the Environment Agency's recorded spill days where the record was usable. It was written down before it was run, on 19 November 2026.

Its figures are not shown. Its check against an earlier rebuild missed the bar set before the run, by one rounded score on one day. And the count for one overflow moved a lot from one season to the next, so a single season's figure would mislead. The season record gives every figure and the diagnosis.

    Questions worth asking next

    proposed, not started

    1. Does the overflow chain add anything to rain at rivers?

      Useful if: samples taken on days fixed before the forecast, at river spots that are not bathing waters, scored against the forecast and against rain alone. Unconvincing if: more model accounting, or samples already read.

    2. Did the Ilkley upgrade change the water at Cromwheel?

      Useful if: the audit statement fixed before any 2026 sample was joined, read once. Its 17 samples make "inconclusive" the likely answer, and it says so. Unconvincing if: a before-and-after of samples with no expectation for rain and season.

    3. Which overflow carries a spot's high days in a season?

      Useful if: a season count whose check passes and whose figures hold between seasons. Unconvincing if: one season's count per overflow, which moved too much between 2024 and 2025.
