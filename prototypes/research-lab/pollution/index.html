What it cannot tell you

  • No spill was followed down a river. The lines are the mapped rivers that join each overflow to the spot. Travel times and weights are the model's settings, fitted to samples, not measured journeys.
  • The clock is an illustration. It shows when water would arrive at the model's assumed speed. Real speed changes with flow: a river in flood moves far faster than one in a dry spell.
  • Monitored overflows only. Farms, misconnected drains, birds, dogs and unmonitored outfalls also carry bacteria. A spot with no overflow upstream is not a clean spot.
  • Sixty kilometres is a cut-off, not a boundary. Overflows further up are left out because the model gives them almost no weight, not because their water never arrives.
  • A lake is not a pipe. At lakes the model holds water for the lake's residence time and mixes it. Wind and currents can carry a plume much faster along one shore.
  • Official advice comes first. Environment Agency notices and signs at the water are shown on each spot page.
